# Positioning the product

The failure this skill prevents: a page written by the people who built the pipeline, in the pipeline's words — "extraction", "grounding", "F1", "calibrated" — that passes every craft critic and tells a visitor nothing. It happened here (chapter 11, 19:20): ten rounds of design critics passed a home page the AI Builder could not make sense of, because the brief critic's goal had been written by the builder, in the builder's language.

## 1. Write `design/positioning.md` first (one page, from the founder framework)

In this order, each in the customer's words, none in the machine's:

1. **Who it is for** — two people at most, by job, with what they do all day.
2. **Their pain, in their words** — four quotes a customer would actually say.
3. **The job to be done** — one sentence beginning with a verb.
4. **The promise** — one sentence a customer would repeat to a colleague. This becomes the headline and the standfirst.
5. **Vision and mission** — one line each; grounded, not inspiring.
6. **Five differentiators, each checkable on the screen** — not adjectives; things a visitor can point at.
7. **Strategic bets** — three lines on what the company believes that competitors do not.
8. **Words we use / words we never use on a customer page** — the never-list is the pipeline's vocabulary.
9. **The proof we can show today, from rows** — numbers in the words a customer uses ("95 of every 100 fields", never "F1 0.95"), including the honest zero.

## 2. Then write the page from it — and only from it

- The eyebrow names the customer. The headline is the promise. The standfirst says what goes in, what comes out, and what happens when the product is not sure.
- Section titles are **what the reader gets**, not what the machine does: "Every number must be found on the page, or it is not trusted", never "Where it looked". (The AI Builder's sample titles its sections "I asked AI to find where I'm losing money" — first person, outcome.)
- Under every plate the scaffold: *what it does, in plain words* · *you need* · *how you know it worked* with a measured number.
- Numbers are sentences: "0 of 60 today, and here is why" beats "0 %".
- One call to action, twice: at the fold and at the end. A pricing hook in words ("plans start free").
- Run the never-list over the finished page: `grep -i` for each word.

## 2b. The home page is a Series-C company's — complete (D-050)

*"The home page should be complete, of a Stage C startup — not some meagre bullshit where I cannot figure out what it is."* Before any prototype is shown, check it against this list; a page missing a row is a lab demo, however good its plates:

- **Navigation** — product, how it works, security, customers, pricing, docs, company; sign in; one primary action.
- **Hero** — the promise in the customer's words, the real product in it (the real specimen), one action, one secondary.
- **Proof** — customer logos and quotes, or the honest substitute stated as such: the measured numbers, the public build, the named limits.
- **What you get** — three outcomes a customer would say back.
- **Product tour** — the sections with plates, each with *what it does · you need · how you know it worked*.
- **Where the numbers go** — integrations with the books and systems the buyer already runs (or "coming" labelled honestly).
- **Security and data** — where documents live, what leaves, what is logged, what an auditor can quote; certifications held or not held, stated.
- **Versus the alternatives** — the two or three things the buyer is weighing (retyping, a cloud API, the incumbent) in one honest table.
- **Pricing teaser** — plans start free; the number a finance lead buys.
- **FAQ** — the questions from the grilling, answered.
- **Footer** — legal, privacy, status, changelog, contact, the open build.

Every claim measured or labelled as not yet true. The visitor critic reads the fold for five seconds and the page for a minute; if they cannot say what the company sells and why it is different, it fails regardless of the critics.

## 3. Then the critics — with the goal written from the visitor's side

The brief critic's goal must be: *a finance lead who has never heard of this lands here; within five seconds they can say what it does, for whom, and what to click; within a minute they can say why it is different and what it will not do.* Never the builder's description of the mechanism. If the AI Builder says they cannot make sense of the page, that verdict outranks every critic (rule 5) and this skill runs again from step 1.

## What breaks this

- Writing the page first and the positioning after (it becomes a caption).
- Letting one pipeline noun through because "it is precise" — precision for the builder is noise for the buyer.
- A proof number without its sentence.
